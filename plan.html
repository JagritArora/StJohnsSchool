Today vs. tomorrow

What changes.

Based on the current site at stjohnsschool.co.in, checked in October 2026.

The current website

  • Latest News, Upcoming Events and Photo Gallery come up empty on the homepage.
  • No phone number, email or full address on the homepage.
  • The browser tab reads “India's Best School ERP Software” — the software vendor's line, not the school's name. This is also what Google shows.
  • Admissions is a page of rules with no way to enquire online.
  • Photos sit only in a slider, without descriptions — invisible to Google and to screen readers.
  • We could not find the CBSE Mandatory Public Disclosure from the homepage.

The new website and app

  • Sixty years of St. John's told properly: founder, values, campus, the stage and the field.
  • Ten clear pages: About, Academics, Admissions, Campus, Student life, Parent corner, News, Contact, and the CBSE disclosure page.
  • Online admission enquiry, straight to the principal's dashboard.
  • News and events posted from the same app teachers use — never empty again.
  • A login for every parent, teacher and the principal: attendance, homework, marks, fees and messages.
  • An AI assistant that answers parents at any hour.
  • Works on any phone, loads fast, light and dark mode, and is easy to find on Google.
How it's used

One school day, in the app.

Teachers enter something once. Parents and the principal see it straight away. Nobody types the same thing twice.

7:20
Parent

Sees the school bus on its route and gets a notification two stops before home.

No more calls to the driver or the office.
8:00
Teacher

Takes roll call on the phone: everyone is present by default, tap the two who aren't. Thirty seconds.

Replaces the paper register, and the monthly totals add themselves.
8:05
Parent

The parent of an absent child gets an app notification and SMS: “Aarav is marked absent today.”

A child who left home but didn't reach school is noticed within minutes.
11:30
Teacher

Posts homework in a free period. AI drafts the practice questions from a topic; the teacher edits and sends.

Every parent sees it. No “my child didn't write it down”.
2:30
Teacher

Enters Unit Test marks once and taps Publish.

Parents see the mark with the class average. Report cards fill in automatically.
8:00 PM
Parent

Asks the assistant “What should Aarav pack for tomorrow?” and gets tomorrow's subjects and pending homework.

The assistant only knows that parent's own child.
Friday
Parent

Gets a short weekly summary: attendance, marks, what's due, what to work on.

Fewer surprises at the PTM.
9:00 AM
Principal

Reads a morning brief: attendance by class, classes that haven't marked, new admission enquiries, fees, students who need support.

The whole school on one screen, without asking for reports.
AI features

AI that drafts, people who decide.

Built on Claude by Anthropic. Every feature below is in the prototype and can be tried.

Website visitors

Admissions assistant

Answers questions about admission, documents, Class XI rules and the campus. If it doesn't know, it passes the question to the office.

Parents

“Ask about my child”

Attendance, marks, homework, fees, bus and notices — for their own child only. A weekly summary arrives on Friday.

Parents

What to work on

Spots the subject that slipped since the last test and suggests simple steps at home before the PTM.

Teachers

Report-card remarks

A personal draft for each student from marks and attendance — all 32 at once. The teacher edits before saving.

Teachers

Practice sets & lesson plans

Worksheets and a 40-minute plan on any topic, sent as homework in one tap.

Teachers & office

Notes to parents

Turns a school notice into a short, warm note for one class, checked and edited by the teacher before sending.

Teachers

Early warning

Flags students with attendance under 85%, a sharp drop in marks, or a subject under 50% — before it shows up in the board result.

Principal

Morning brief

A short daily summary of attendance, admissions, fees and students needing support.

Principal

Ask the school

“Which class has the lowest attendance this month?” — answered from live records.

Safeguards

  • Drafts, not decisions. Nothing written by AI reaches a parent or a report card until a teacher approves it.
  • Each person sees only their own part. A parent's assistant can only read that parent's child; a teacher's, only their classes.
  • No training on student data. Data sent to Claude through Anthropic's commercial service is not used to train their models.
  • It says when it doesn't know, and passes the question to the office instead of guessing.
Children's data

Privacy built in from day one.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets stricter rules for children's data. The system is designed around them.

Parent consent

Parents give consent when they first sign in, and can see what is stored about their child.

No ads, no tracking

No advertising, no third-party trackers and no behavioural profiling of children anywhere in the app or website.

Stored in India

Records live on servers in India, with role-based access and a log of who changed what.

Existing software

Works with what the school already has.

Today's website runs on Entab's CampusCare. There are two ways forward, and the school can choose after Phase 1.

Connect

Keep CampusCare for fees and records; the new website and app read from it. Least disruption for the office. Depends on Entab giving access to the data.

Replace

Move everything into the new system: one login and one place for every record, and no separate software subscription.

Rollout

Live in about twelve weeks.

Each phase is usable on its own, so the school sees value from the first month.

Weeks 1–3

New website

All pages, photos, news, admission enquiry form, Google listing, CBSE disclosure page.

Weeks 4–7

Parent & teacher app

Attendance, homework, notices, messages, leave requests. Teacher training sessions.

Weeks 8–10

Marks & fees

Marks entry, report cards, online fee payment by UPI with instant receipts.

Weeks 11–12

AI assistant

Ask St. John's, weekly summaries, remarks, practice sets, morning brief.
